The FireSmart Rebate: Up to $5,000 in the Boundary — and the One Mistake That Costs You All of It

July 21, 2026

There is real money available right now to help Boundary homeowners reduce the wildfire risk around their homes. Not a tax credit, not a vague promise — a rebate of between $500 and $5,000 from the Regional District of Kootenay Boundary, paid out on work done around your house.

Most people either have not heard of it or assume it is more trouble than it is worth. It is not. But there is one rule that catches people out, and it is worth putting before anything else.

The mistake: doing the work first

You must have a free FireSmart home assessment before you do the work. Not after. Not "while we were already up there anyway." Before.

Only work that was recommended in your assessment, is on the program's eligible-activities list, sits within 30 metres of your home, and was completed after the assessment counts toward a rebate. The RDKB's own application form puts it about as plainly as a government document ever does: "No exceptions will be made."

So if you spend $6,000 clearing conifers back from the house next month and then go looking for the rebate, you get nothing. Same work, same cost, same reduction in risk — but the order was wrong, so there is no money, and there is nothing anyone can do about it after the fact.

Book the assessment first. It is free, and it is the entire gate.

What you can actually get back

The rebate is a 50% cost-share, with a minimum of $500 and a maximum of $5,000. In practice that means:

  • $1,000 of qualifying work gets you $500 back
  • $10,000 or more of qualifying work gets you the $5,000 maximum

Your own labour counts too, valued at $32.00 per hour, so you are not required to hire anybody. You can do the whole thing yourself, hire it out, or mix the two — the application form has a column for each.

Within that overall $5,000 ceiling, the program caps what it will pay out by area of your property:

  • On the home itself — up to $5,000. Fire-rated roofing, non-combustible gutters, ember-resistant vents and 3 mm screening, ignition-resistant siding, tempered glass, sealing gaps under decks. Clearing needles, leaves and overhanging branches is capped at $500.
  • Immediate zone, 0–1.5 m — up to $4,000. A non-combustible surface right against the house, and no vegetation at all in that strip — no shrubs, no grass, no trees.
  • Intermediate zone, 1.5–10 m — up to $3,000. This is the big one for tree work. Removing conifers, or keeping them and limbing up to 2 m with 3 m of crown spacing. Replacing conifers and hedges with fire-resistant deciduous species. Moving woodpiles beyond 10 m.
  • Extended zone, 10–30 m — up to $2,000. Thinning, creating separation between conifer crowns, and removing low branches within 2 m of the ground.

Those caps can be combined up to the $5,000 total. One condition worth knowing: the work cannot be predominantly out in that extended zone. The program wants the money spent close to the house, because that is where it changes the outcome.

So, does tree work qualify?

Yes — and it is named specifically, not left to interpretation. Coniferous tree removal in the 1.5–10 m zone is the first item listed under trees. Limbing mature conifers to 2 m and establishing 3 m crown spacing qualifies. Out in the 10–30 m zone, thinning and removing low branches within 2 m of the ground qualifies.

That is a large share of what we do all summer. The difference is that with an assessment and an approved work plan in hand, half of it can come back to you.

How to actually get it — four steps

  1. Book the free assessment. An RDKB Wildfire Mitigation Specialist walks your property and writes up recommendations specific to it. Book at emergency.rdkb.com/FireSmart, email firesmart@rdkb.com, or call 236-968-3873.
  2. Get a Rebate Work Plan approved before you start. You build this with the same specialist. If you had an assessment in a previous year you do not need a new one, but you do need a 2026 work plan.
  3. Do the work and document it. Track your hours, keep every receipt, and take before-and-after photos. Applications over $1,000 get a follow-up verification visit, so the photos matter.
  4. Submit. Email the completed application to firesmart@rdkb.com.

Applications are open until October 31, 2026, and it is first-come, first-served while the funding lasts. One rebate per home per year.

Check whether your property qualifies

Eligibility depends on exactly where your property sits. The rebate follows regional district boundaries, not the area we service. Some communities in the region run their own separate FireSmart programs with different rebate amounts, and a few addresses fall outside this program altogether.

So do not guess, and do not assume that what your neighbour was told applies to you. Before you plan any work, confirm your own address with the FireSmart office:

  • In the Kootenay Boundary — email firesmart@rdkb.com, call 236-968-3873, or start at emergency.rdkb.com/FireSmart.
  • In Osoyoos — start with the Town's FireSmart program at firesmart@osoyoos.ca or 250-485-7625.

Ask them two things: whether your address is covered, and what the maximum rebate is where you live. One phone call settles it, and it is the difference between planning around $5,000 and planning around nothing.
